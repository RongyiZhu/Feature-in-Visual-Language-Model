\begin{abstract}
We introduce a framework for extracting visual category directions from
frozen vision--language models through matched image interventions.
Visual tokens have no discrete identities, and an object's pixel location
need not reveal which hidden states encode its category. We address this
problem by inserting target and competitor objects into a shared scene,
weighting all visual tokens by their responses to the interventions, and
averaging the resulting paired contrasts. A cosine readout with top-fraction
pooling evaluates transfer to disjoint natural images without fitting a
classifier on evaluation labels. Controlled ablations on Qwen2.5-VL-3B show
that paired subtraction improves macro presence AUC by 7.88 percentage
points over subtracting the unedited image on a 10-category collection.
With 32 fitting records per category, increasing the competitor count from
two to eight improves AUC by 0.81 and 1.38 points on the eligible cohorts
of two discovery collections. Equal competitor weighting has smaller,
inconsistent effects. These results support paired contrast estimation and
broad competitor coverage for recovering transferable category information.
\end{abstract}
