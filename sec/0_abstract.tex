\begin{abstract}
Interpreting the internal representations of vision-language models (VLMs)
is challenging because visual tokens, unlike text tokens, have no discrete
identities. Existing methods often explain these representations through
words or captions, making their conclusions dependent on a noisy naming step
and limiting their ability to capture visual concepts such as attributes and
relational binding. We introduce a framework for identifying and evaluating
visual features across multiple levels of abstraction. It derives feature
directions from controlled visual interventions rather than textual
descriptions and evaluates them with a calibrated, label-free metric.
Applying this framework to open-weight VLMs from multiple model families, we are able to access the interpretability of VLMs. our
results identify where linear feature structure remains accessible in VLM
representations and where it breaks down.
\end{abstract}
